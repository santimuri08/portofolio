# Persuasion Principles (Applied to Portfolios)

Drawn from Cialdini's influence research, adapted for a portfolio context
where the "ask" is: take this candidate seriously.

## Principles in Use

### Micro-yes
Small, easy commitments compound into larger ones. A visitor who nods at
the hero claim is more likely to click a project. A visitor who finishes
one case study is more likely to email. Each element should earn a small yes.

### Social Proof
People trust what others have already trusted. On a portfolio this means:
real company names, real shipped products, real GitHub commits, real LinkedIn.
Testimonials if they exist. Not "I'm passionate about" language.

### Authority
Credibility signals. For an emerging product engineer: clean code on GitHub,
shipped projects at real URLs, case studies that explain decisions (not just
describe features), evidence of taste.

### Congruence
Every element agrees with every other element. If the hero says "considered
design," the spacing must be considered. If the archetype is Explorer + Creator,
the projects should show range and craft, not just one kind of work.
A single incongruent element (stock photo, cliché copy) breaks the whole spell.

### Restraint as Credential
What you leave out signals taste as much as what you include. A portfolio
with three strong projects beats one with ten mediocre projects. Blank space
beats filler copy. "I ship" beats a skills soup of every framework.

## Application to This Portfolio

- **Micro-yes:** Hero claim is specific enough to nod at, not vague enough to shrug
- **Social proof:** Real project URLs, real GitHub, real LinkedIn
- **Authority:** Case studies show decision-making, not feature lists
- **Congruence:** Calibrated restraint in copy, layout, palette, and project selection
- **Restraint:** 5–7 projects max; no "other projects I dabbled in"

## Anti-patterns

- Vague claims ("passionate about crafting experiences")
- Social proof without evidence (logo wall with no case study)
- Authority by volume (dumping every side project)
- Incongruence (minimalist layout, maximalist copy)
